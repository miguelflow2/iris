In testing

Where we stand, frankly. Available in IRIS 0.1.0: opening an app or a website from the text chat (commands in French), routines and reminders, with a French voice produced on the computer. In testing: “Dis-moi Iris” and voice commands, because once your glasses are paired, IRIS 0.1.0 only listens to you while they are connected, and that link is in testing; dictation, search, screen reading and control by label, which go through IRIS Online. The full table.

IRIS is built in French: the app’s interface is in French, and its everyday commands are understood in French. Its settings offer English (US): the English speech-recognition model then has to be downloaded once (about 40 MB). IRIS has no built-in English voice: its offline voice is French, and in the settings you can switch to the Windows voice, your computer’s own.

Why local helps

Your data stays under your control.

An accessibility tool sees a lot of your life. At VELA, it is stored at home with you.

  • Memory, history and log are stored on your computer. In local mode, your data stays on your computer.
  • The French voice and wake-word detection work without Internet.
  • When you turn on an online feature, IRIS shows you what will be sent before it goes. In practice, in IRIS 0.1.0: one consent per type of data, off by default, a preview before a screenshot is attached, and these sends recorded in the log, which you verify yourself.
  • Outside local mode, IRIS also contacts VELA’s server at startup, with a device identifier (and your purchase email, if you entered it), to open online access; that contact is not recorded in the log. The 100% local mode blocks it.
  • The trade-off, and we own it: without a computer that is switched on and within range, the glasses don’t drive IRIS today.
