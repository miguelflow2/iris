The proof, not the promise

A log you verify yourself.

IRIS records in a log, on your computer, consent and mode changes, purges, sends to the online service and other actions. Each entry carries a SHA-256 hash that includes the hash of the previous entry: changing or removing a line after the fact breaks the chain, and the verification shows it — unless someone recalculates every hash that follows. That is why a final hash published separately matters. Two blocks below: a demo of the principle, then a log produced by IRIS during a scripted session.

Your computer is the brain

In local mode, your data stays on your computer.

When you turn on an online feature, IRIS shows you what will be sent before it goes. In practice, in IRIS 0.1.0: one consent per type of data, off by default, a preview before a screenshot is attached, and the send recorded in the log. The trade-off, and we own it: without a computer that is switched on and within range, the glasses don’t drive IRIS today.

What is sent, and what doesn’t go through your consents. Your requests, attached images, screenshots and memories aren’t sent without your consent, type by type, and these sends are recorded in the log. Outside local mode, IRIS also contacts VELA’s server at startup, with a device identifier (and your purchase email, if you entered it), to open online access, then once a day to check your plan if that email is entered; these contacts are not recorded in the log. The 100% local mode blocks them.

In testing

No telemetry in the app

The app sends no telemetry and no usage statistics. If you use IRIS Online, our server counts your requests to apply the quota, without keeping your requests (see the policy, in French). By default, IRIS’s local service only listens on your machine, behind a token regenerated at every startup; if you turn on access from your phone, it also listens on your local network, behind a password.

Take them with you, or erase them

The log exports to JSON or CSV. The log is cleared whenever you decide; that clearing is not itself recorded. The purge permanently deletes data that has reached the end of the period you chose; memories are erased one by one.

Four safeguards

What you stay in control of.

Each one is visible in the app and reversible; consent and mode changes are recorded in the log.

Consent, by type

Text, audio, images, screenshots, memory: all off by default. No online send of that type without your consent; each switch can be turned off in one gesture.

The capture indicator

When the computer’s mic, screen or a camera is in use, a badge appears on screen. The glasses’ frame also has an indicator light linked to its camera.

Private mode

One gesture cuts the mic. The 100% local mode, for its part, blocks sends to online services: IRIS keeps its voice and the wake word, which work on the computer.

On your drive, for as long as you choose

Memories, messages and logs stay on your device. You set the retention period and purge whenever you see fit.

The glasses’ camera

Photos by voice are in testing.

IRIS 0.1.0 still refuses to trigger the glasses’ camera until the command is confirmed on the real hardware In testing. What is written in the software for that day: the image is brought back to your computer, and the capture is recorded in the log. We will only call it “Available” once it has been verified.

So what about always-on listening?

To hear “Dis-moi Iris”, it does have to listen. So the real question isn’t “is it listening?” but “where does what it hears go?”.

IRIS spots “Dis-moi Iris” on your computer, with a French speech-recognition model installed with the app: that sound is not sent online. For IRIS Online to answer, the text of your request is transmitted — and, depending on your consents, attached images, screenshots or memory excerpts. These sends need your consent and IRIS records them in the log. The test session published above contains no online send: it doesn’t demonstrate this point.

The details, mechanism by mechanism, are in the privacy policy (in French).
