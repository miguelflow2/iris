The proof, not the promise

A log you can verify yourself.

Every capture and every transmission is written to a log, on your device. You can check its integrity in one click: if a line had been altered or removed, the verification would fail. This isn't a promise about the design — it's proof of what actually ran.

Local processing by default

Nothing goes to a cloud by default.

IRIS runs on your PC. The glasses connect to it over Bluetooth. Everything happens on your computer: that's the trade-off we accept for "everything stays with you".

Nothing comes back to us

No telemetry, no usage statistics, no automatic uploads. The IRIS local service only listens on your machine, behind a token regenerated at each startup.

You take it with you, or erase it

The log exports to JSON or CSV, and clears whenever you decide. Your memories can be edited one at a time; a purge removes the data for good.

Four safeguards

What you keep control over.

No hidden settings. Each one is visible, reversible, and written to the log when it changes.

Consent, by type

Text, audio, images, captures, memory: nothing goes out without your agreement for that specific type. Each switch can be turned off with a single tap.

The capture indicator

As soon as the mic, the screen or the camera are in use, an indicator appears. A light also turns on the glasses when the camera records: you always know.

Private mode

One gesture cuts the mic. The 100% local mode relies on an offline English voice (Piper) and local speech recognition (Vosk): nothing goes out anymore.

On your disk, for as long as you choose

Memories, messages and logs stay on your device. You set how long they're kept and you purge whenever you see fit.

The capture indicator

An indicator lights up as soon as the camera records.

Nothing happens in secret. The request, the action, the trace: every step is visible, and the last one is written to the log you can verify.

So what about always-on listening?

To hear "Dis-moi Iris", it does have to listen. So the real question isn't "is it listening?" but "where does what it hears go?".

Speech recognition can run entirely on your device, with a local model (French is built in; the English model downloads once). In that case, no sound goes out. And for an online AI service to answer, only the text of your request is transmitted — with your agreement, and written to the log every time.

The detail, mechanism by mechanism, is in the privacy policy.
